The problem

Why it exists

Checking someone’s vital signs by hand is slow and repetitive: measure, write it down, type it in later. Vitalyze lets a person walk up, tap their ID, and be measured while the numbers go straight to a dashboard.

How it works

One visit, five steps

A person taps their ID, gets measured, and sees the result. Each step is handled by a different sensor.

  1. Tap your ID

    The kiosk knows who is being measured.

    RFID reader RC522 · board A

  2. Heart rate

    Rest a finger on the sensor window.

    MAX30102 · board A

  3. Body temperature

    A contactless reading from the forehead, 5–10 cm away.

    MLX90614 · board B

  4. Height and weight

    Stand on the platform: a sensor above measures height, a load cell measures weight.

    HC-SR04 · board D · HX711 · board E

  5. Results

    Readings go to the backend and show up on the admin dashboard.

    NestJS · MySQL · React

Meanwhile board C watches room temperature and smoke and runs the ventilation fans. [Confirm the order of the steps and where the results are shown to the user.]

    Challenges & solutions

    Problems I solved

    Two sensors, same pins

    THE PROBLEM
    The heart-rate sensor and the RFID reader had to share one board, but both wanted the same two pins.
    WHAT I DID
    Kept the heart-rate sensor on its dedicated I2C pins and moved the RFID reader’s select and reset lines to GPIO 25 and 26.
    THE RESULT
    One board runs both, and the layout passes the automated pin and overlap checks with 0 errors.

    Power from a laptop charger

    THE PROBLEM
    The adapter I had was 19 V, but the fans and boards were designed for 12 V.
    WHAT I DID
    Added a 19 V to 12 V buck converter at the input, re-rated the surge protection for 19 V, and kept everything after it unchanged.
    THE RESULT
    Calculated worst-case draw is about 1.4 A at 19 V, roughly 30% of what the adapter can supply. [Add the measured value after bench testing.]

    Keeping one fault from taking down everything

    THE PROBLEM
    Five boards on one supply: a short or a surge on one could stop the whole kiosk.
    WHAT I DID
    Gave every board its own resettable fuse, its own 5 V regulator, surge and reverse-polarity protection, and a 5 V monitor pin.
